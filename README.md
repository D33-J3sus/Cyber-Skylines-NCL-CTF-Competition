# Cyber-Skylines-NCL-CTF-Competition-Cybersecurity-Portfolio-Practicum
The National Cyber Leage CTF competition hosted by CyberSkylines has allowed me necessary exposure to skillsets and scenarios within cybersecurity, thus allowing me to build technical competency critical for achieving my career goals. 

I participated in my first NCL competition after opting into a pilot group for the portfolio practicum course at the Sans Technology Institute. At this point in time, I had been working in cybersecurity for approximately 4 years, across various roles like level 1 SOC analyst, JR Penetration Tester, and Vulnerability Management Analyst. Having had diverse experience within the field, I had finally come to determine what I found most interesting, which was DFIR work. 
I opted into the experienced student cohort for my first season, which was an incredibly humbling experience, as I found the technical rigor of the challenges to be substantial. That being said, I also enjoyed the exhilarating feeling of making progress in the Forensics, Log Analysis, and Network Traffic Analysis sections of the competitions. 

I started making noticeable improvement in the 2025 Spring season, where I placed 159/602 (26th percentile) in the Forensics category in the Individual Game, and helped my team place 83/457 (18th percentile) by completing forensic, log analysis, packet analysis, and malware investigation challenges within the Team Game. 
Experiences like these helped me build the confidence and competence needed to transition from my former role as Vulnerability Management Analyst, and secure a DFIR role as a Principle Defense Ops Specialist. 

I continue to enjoy participating in the NCL individual and team game competitions at a relaxed, non-competitive pace. My main goal every season is to learn something new, and walk away with memorable anecdotes of scenarios and challenges I solved, as well as helping contribute a positive impact in a team based setting.  

Competition Highlights 2026: One challenge involved performing deep dive file analysis of a word document. The pretext of the challenge was that a suspicious word document was sent to a Finance department with the pretext of being an invoice, and competitors were put into the shoes of the SOC analyst charged with determining its impact. Using tools like strings, DD, DDX, FT, and CyberChef, I was able to find multiple IOCs used to solve challenge questions. Finally, after carving out a file object embedded in the word document, a file containing a Base64 encoded flag was found.
